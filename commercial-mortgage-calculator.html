What a commercial lender actually checks

Commercial lending works differently from a residential mortgage. A residential lender underwrites you. A commercial lender underwrites the property, and asks whether it can service the debt on its own.

Loan to value

Most commercial lenders will go to 70% of value, a few to 75%. Above that the options narrow sharply and pricing rises. An investment property let on a long lease to a strong covenant will stretch further than an owner-occupied unit in a weak location.

Interest cover

Rent divided by your annual mortgage payments. Lenders typically want at least 1.25, so the rent covers the payments with a quarter to spare. The part that catches people out is that most now stress this at two or three percentage points above the rate you are actually paying — which is why a deal that works today can still be declined.

Stamp duty

Commercial property uses a lower scale than residential. Nothing below £150,000, then 2% to £250,000, then 5% above that. The figure above assumes a freehold purchase in England or Northern Ireland; Scotland and Wales have their own regimes.

Why the term matters more than people expect

Stretching a loan from 15 years to 25 cuts the monthly payment and lifts the interest cover, which can be the difference between an offer and a decline. It also costs considerably more in total interest. Change the term above and watch both move.
